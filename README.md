## Entry

Section 7.1: The Company reserves the right to require the User to applaud upon successful login, such applause to be audible, enthusiastic, and not less than three (3) seconds in duration.

## Why this wins

This clause works because it never breaks character. It opens with pure terms-of-service boilerplate — "The Company reserves the right to require the User" — the exact phrasing that makes eyes glaze over in a real agreement. Then the absurdity lands as one concrete, physical act: the user must applaud every time they log in. What makes it funny is the legal precision piled on top — the applause must be "audible, enthusiastic, and not less than three (3) seconds in duration," as if a compliance team somewhere had calibrated exactly how much applause a login deserves. It is one tight sentence, original and specific, funny on a single reading with no outside setup, and it could genuinely sit buried at Section 7.1 of a 40-page agreement without anyone noticing until their first login.
